# Fulcrum

The Fulcrum is a concept of a point in front of you that all strikes move through. You won’t find many references about it in martial art manuals, but it is there between the lines. In longsword it is referred to as the primary gate. In lightsaber, our target areas include the head, torso, upper arms and upper legs. So, we have three fulcrums to cover.

<Picture src={fulcrumOverview} alt="The 3 fulcrum points (high, mid, low) relative to a battle droid" densities={[1.5, 2]} class="max-h-[40vh] mb-8" fit="contain" formats={['avif', 'webp']} layout="scale-down" />

## Covering your fulcrums
So how do we cover our Fulcrums? We also have three main ways to achieve this.

The first way to cover your Fulcrums is to simply hold your saber in such a way that your opponent will hit the weapon first, before striking through the Fulcrum.

The second way is to hold your saber ready to cover the Fulcrum, basically exposing the Fulcrum to a strike from your opponent but positioned such that you will quickly be able to cover it.

And thirdly, you can cover your own Fulcrum, by taking control of your opponent’s Fulcrum. This will force him to first take care of his own safety and not attack you.

## Fulcrums are relative
One thing you must take into account is that how you cover or strike through fulcrums is relative to your enemy. Yours are relative to _you_, but theirs are relative to _them_.

<Picture src={fulcrumHeights} alt="The relative lines connecting the fulcrums between opponents of different sizes" densities={[1.5, 2]} class="max-h-[40vh] mb-5" fit="contain" formats={['avif', 'webp']} layout="scale-down" />

Depending on the opponent's stance or movements, they can even (temporarily) become unavailable. For example, when crouching down low, the middle fulcrum might temporarily dissappear.
